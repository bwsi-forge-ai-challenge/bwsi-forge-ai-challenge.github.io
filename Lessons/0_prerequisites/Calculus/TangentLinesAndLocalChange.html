Tangent Lines and Local Change

To begin studying calculus, we first need to understand an important geometric idea: the tangent line.

When looking at a curved graph, it may be difficult to describe how the graph is behaving at one exact point. A tangent line helps us zoom in locally and approximate the curve with a straight line.

1. What Is a Tangent Line?

A tangent line is a straight line that touches a curve at a specific point and follows the direction of the curve at that location.

If you zoom in closely enough on a smooth curve, the curve begins to look almost straight near that point. The tangent line represents this local straight-line behavior.

In other words, we can examine the "slope" of a continuous function at a point with a tangent line.
