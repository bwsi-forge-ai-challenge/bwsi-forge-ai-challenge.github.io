Generative AI Use Policy

In this course, we do not just study Artificial Intelligence systems, we actively build them. Because of that, we encourage you to use generative AI tools as a meaningful part of your workflow. At the same time, we want to protect your learning, preserve authentic collaboration, and maintain clear accountability. The following guidelines are designed to help us strike that balance and set expectations.

Coding & Development: Full Speed Ahead

We encourage the use of AI coding assistants, agents, and large language models for programming tasks. Navigating complex APIs, debugging difficult syntax, scaffolding projects, and accelerating iteration are exactly the kinds of tasks these tools excel at. Learning to work effectively with AI-assisted development is an important modern engineering skill.

Guidelines:

  • Give credit: Use workflows or tools (such as Claude Code) that automatically append AI attribution to your Git commit messages whenever possible.
  • Stay accountable: Some assignments will require you to document how you used AI tools during development so we can better understand your learning process and technical decision-making.
  • Understand your work: If you submit code, you should be able to explain how it works, why it works, and what tradeoffs or design choices were involved.

Human-to-Human Communication

While AI can be extremely useful for coding and technical workflows, human connection is the foundation of this cohort. Because of that, we draw a much stricter line around written communication intended for other people.

You are welcome to use AI as a private brainstorming partner, proofreading tool, or assistant for generating standardized technical documentation. However, do not use AI to write substantive communication that is meant to represent your own thoughts or voice.

This includes:

  • Piazza and discussion forum posts
  • Peer review feedback and comments
  • Project reflections and personal writeups
  • Design documents and final reports

When communication is filtered through a generic corporate AI tone, we lose individual voice, genuine discussion, and trust within the cohort. Put simply: do not ask your classmates or instructors to spend time reading something you did not spend time thinking through yourself.

Ownership Rule

You own your output

No matter what tools you use, you are fully responsible for your final output.

An AI system cannot take responsibility for a bug, a flawed design decision, inaccurate information, or academic misconduct. If your name is on a submission, you are responsible for understanding and standing behind it.

Our Commitment

We hold ourselves to these standards as well.

We will not use generative AI to write our core announcements, personalized feedback, or direct communication with students. While we may use AI tools behind the scenes for formatting, brainstorming, organization, or administrative support, the guidance, mentorship, and feedback you receive will come directly from us.
