Written report structure

  1. Your styles and why.
  2. Data and datasheets.
  3. Per-stage methods, including the special technique each member chose and why.
  4. Results for E1–E5 with figures and confidence intervals.
  5. The failure gallery.
  6. What you'd do with another two weeks.

Then one page per member, written alone: the decision you're proudest of, the one you'd undo, and what the telephone game taught you about multimodal models.

Video report rules

  • Every member appears on camera or speaks in their own voice for at least 2 minutes, explaining their own stages, data and choices.
  • Must include: real outputs from your runs (play the music), a screen recording of one chain running end to end, and a 30-second "telephone reel" following one seed image through a full E3 chain.
  • Slides, script and narration are made by you. No AI-generated slides, voice-over or script, and no AI-generated background music beyond your own models' outputs shown as results.

Live demo + oral check

Staff bring a new image and an ordering. You run the chain live and talk through the outputs. Then each member answers 1–2 questions about their own code, data or results (for example "why rank 16?", "show me where your L training data is mixed", "what would happen if we removed your caption dropout?"). Answers count toward individual grades.

Optional bonus: class-wide telephone. Teams that keep the shared interface can join a final-session chain that passes one image across every team in the class.

2. Timeline

The project runs 14 days in four phases; the Day 7 integration check is the gate that matters most.

At the Day 7 check, staff confirm every member's four stages run through the shared interface (even if not yet well-tuned) and read the Part A peer check-ins. Teams that miss it meet with staff on Day 8 to rescope.

4. AI-Use Policy

AI coding assistants are allowed for code, with a log; everything that carries your style, your data, your judgment or your voice must be made by you. This project's rules add to the course Generative AI Use Policy.

Allowed (log it)Not allowed
Coding assistants for boilerplate, debugging, data loaders, plottingUndisclosed synthetic data (AI-generated images, music or captions) in any training or held-out set
Asking an AI to explain an error or a librarySynthetic data in held-out or human-study sets, even if disclosed

AI-use log. Each member submits a short table: tool, what you used it for, which files. You must be able to explain and modify any line of your code in the oral check. Code you can't explain is graded as code you didn't write.

5. Ethics, Consent, and Licensing

You are building music and image style models, so consent and licensing rules are part of the grade, not a footnote.

  • Datasets and licenses. Use public research datasets, public-domain collections or openly licensed media, and log each source's license and terms in your datasheet. Some datasets only link to web content (MusicCaps, Conceptual Captions); don't redistribute what you can't.
  • Style targets. No prompting or training to imitate a named living artist, illustrator, singer or band, and no copyrighted characters or logos as style targets.
  • People in images. Prefer seed and style images without identifiable private individuals, and drop any you come across that look sensitive.
  • Content. Keep safety filters on for G. If a chain produces something offensive or harmful, stop it, note it in your report as a finding, and don't put it in the gallery or video.
  • Rating study. Raters are classmates. Keep forms anonymous and don't collect personal data beyond what the study needs.
  • Model licenses. Some base models (for example MusicGen weights, Stable Audio Open) carry non-commercial or community licenses. That's fine for coursework; note it in your README.
  • Report section. Include one paragraph on how style fine-tuning could be misused (imitating a working musician or illustrator) and what safeguards you'd want before releasing a system like yours.

Each member fills this in confidentially twice: a short check-in on Day 7 (Part A only) and the full form on Day 14.

  2. Is anyone blocked, missing, or carrying too much? (free text, seen only by staff)

Part C: Contribution split

Divide 100 points across all team members, including yourself, to reflect overall contribution. An even split is fine if it's true.

Part D: Open questions

  1. For each teammate: one thing they did that the team couldn't have done without.
  2. For each teammate: one specific thing they could do better next time.
  3. Your own contribution: what you built, roughly how many hours you spent, and what you'd do differently.
  4. Anything staff should know? (confidential)
