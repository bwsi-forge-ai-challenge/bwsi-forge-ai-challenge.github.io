The Sculptor in the Blizzard: Diffusion Basics

GANs generate an image in one forward pass from noise. Diffusion models take the opposite journey: start from pure noise and peel it away, step by step, until structure appears. The idea feels slow — but it is remarkably stable and now powers DALL·E 3, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney.

1. Forward diffusion: the blizzard arrives

Metaphor: A marble statue stands in a courtyard. A blizzard blows in, coating it with thicker and thicker static each hour. After enough time you cannot tell there was ever a face — only white noise. Forward diffusion is that corruption schedule, but mathematically controlled.

Given a clean image x₀, forward diffusion adds Gaussian noise over T timesteps until xT ≈ random noise. Each step uses a small variance βt so the signal fades smoothly.

q(xt | xt−1) = 𝒩(xt; √(1 − βt) · xt−1, βt · I)

In code you rarely loop one pixel at a time; you can jump straight to any timestep with a closed form using cumulative products ᾱt. But the story is always: signal → noise, monotonically.

x₀ clean image shape (H, W, 3) → x₁ slightly noised → x₂ more noise ... → x_T ~ pure Gaussian noise

Check your knowledge: direction

Question: Forward diffusion goes from image to noise. What does the generative (sampling) process do?

Show answer

Reverse diffusion: start from noise xT and iteratively denoise to x₀. Training teaches a network to undo one small noise step at a time — like the sculptor chipping away static to reveal the figure underneath.

2. Reverse diffusion: the sculptor chips static away

At sampling time we do not have a clean image — only noise. A learned model (historically a U-Net, now often a transformer variant) looks at the noisy image xt and timestep t, then predicts the noise component ε that was added. Subtract a scaled version and you land closer to x₀.

xt−1 ≈ (1 / √(1 − βt)) · (xt − (βt / √(1 − ᾱt)) · εθ(xt, t))

Do not memorize the fractions — notice the pattern: current noisy state + noise prediction + schedule = slightly cleaner state. Repeat for t = T … 1.

Worked numbers (T = 1000). Suppose at step t = 500 the network predicts noise εθ with mean magnitude 0.4 (in normalized pixel space). The sampler scales that prediction by schedule constants (often ~0.02–0.05 at mid-t) and subtracts it from x500. After one step you might move 5% closer to the clean image in L2 sense — tiny per step, but 500 steps compound into a crisp circle or face. Production models use fast samplers (DDIM, DPM++) to cut 1000 network calls down to 20–50 with similar quality.

Check your knowledge: training target

Question: During training we take a real image, add noise to get xt, and ask the network to predict something. Is the target the clean image x₀ or the noise ε?

Show answer

Standard DDPM training predicts the noise ε that was used to corrupt the image (equivalent formulations predict x₀ or velocity — same idea, different algebra). Loss is usually MSE between predicted and actual noise — a regression problem, not classification.

3. Latent diffusion: denoise small, decode big

Full 512×512 RGB diffusion is expensive. Stable Diffusion runs the blizzard in a VAE latent — the compressed map from Lesson 1 — then decodes once at the end.

sample z_T ~ 𝒩(0, I) latent noise, shape ~(64, 64, 4) for t = T … 1: ε = U-Net(z_t, t) predict noise in latent space z_{t-1} = denoise_step(z_t, ε, t) x₀ = VAE_decode(z₀) RGB image (512, 512, 3)

Lesson 4 will add text embeddings into that U-Net call — for now, notice the pipeline: noise → iterative denoise in latent → VAE decode → pixels. No adversarial forger required.

Check your knowledge: GAN vs diffusion

Question: A GAN generates in one shot from z. A diffusion model needs hundreds of steps. Why might researchers accept that cost?

Show answer

Diffusion training is typically more stable (no min–max tug-of-war), scales well with data and compute, and gives fine control over diversity via the noise schedule. Fast samplers and latent space shrink the step-count penalty — trade one-shot speed for reliability and quality.
