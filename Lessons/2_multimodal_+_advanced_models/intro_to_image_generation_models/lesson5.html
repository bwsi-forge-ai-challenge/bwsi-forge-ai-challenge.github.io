1. Why structural logic fails

Diffusion models learn local co-occurrence statistics from billions of image–caption pairs: “skin-colored blobs near rectangles often appear in portraits,” “parallel lines near wheels often mean car.” They do not build an internal 3D skeleton or hand anatomy graph.

FailureWhat you seeWhy it happens
Hands & fingers6 fingers, fused digits, floating handsFingers are small, occluded, varied in pose — weak signal vs. faces; model stitches plausible skin patches.
Text in imagesGarbled letters, nonsense signageLetters need precise global layout; model predicts texture that looks like text from training captions, not spellings.
Counting“Three apples” → four or merged blobsNo discrete counter; “three” is a language token, not three separate object slots.
Left/right, mirrorsAsymmetric eyes, wrong eyeglass armBilateral symmetry is not enforced; reflections are learned as texture.
PhysicsImpossible shadows, floating objectsLighting is correlated pixels, not ray tracing.
Key insight: High CFG (Lesson 4) can make failures more visible — the model commits harder to wrong structure. More prompt adjectives ≠ true reasoning.

Check your knowledge: six fingers

Question: Why do extra fingers appear even with a detailed prompt like “anatomically correct five-finger hand”?

Show answer

The model has no explicit finger counter or 3D rig — it paints statistically likely skin/edge textures. “Five fingers” is one token among many; local patches can still merge or duplicate digits especially at small scale.

2. ControlNet — structural anchoring

ControlNet (and similar adapters) feeds extra spatial conditions into the denoiser alongside text: edge maps (Canny), human pose skeletons, depth maps, segmentation masks, scribbles. The trainable side network copies U-Net/DiT weights and learns to respect those structures while text still controls style and content.

reference photo or drawing → preprocessor (OpenPose / MiDaS / Canny / …) → control map (same spatial size as latent pathway) → ControlNet branch + base denoiser → cross-attn to text embeddings (still!) → denoise loop → decode
  • Pose ControlNet: lock body layout; fix “person melting into background.”
  • Depth ControlNet: preserve foreground/background ordering.
  • Canny edges: trace a line drawing; fill with prompt-driven texture.
When to reach for ControlNet vs. prompt only:
TaskPrompt only+ ControlNet
“Person waving, exact pose from storyboard”Random pose each seedPose map anchors limbs
“Room layout like this sketch”Walls driftEdge/depth map locks geometry
“Same cat, new outfit”Identity shiftsSegmentation + inpainting helps

3. Dataset bias — the hidden prompt

Every model carries priors from training data — often Western stock photos, anime subsets, celebrity faces, and caption noise. Your explicit prompt fights an implicit prompt baked into weights.

Bias patternSymptomMitigation (partial)
Profession stereotypes“Doctor” → older man in coatSpecific descriptors; diverse reference images + ControlNet
Beauty defaultsSame face shape across seedsVaried ethnicities/features in prompt; different models
Landscape clichésMountains + lake + sunsetNegative prompts; unusual locations
Caption spamWatermarks, “artstation” lookNegative prompts; finetunes without junk
Prompt engineering can reduce bias visibility but cannot erase training skew. Responsible use means noticing who is missing or stereotyped in outputs.

7. Ethics brief — paint with responsibility

Deepfakes & consent: Swapping a real person’s face or mimicking their likeness without permission can harm reputation and safety. Many jurisdictions now regulate non-consensual synthetic media.

Training data: Models learn from scraped web images — artists’ work, private photos, and copyrighted characters may appear in outputs. “I typed it, I own it” is legally and ethically unsettled.

Misinformation: Photorealistic fakes of events that never happened erode trust. Label AI-generated media when sharing; do not present synthetic evidence as real.

Classroom norm: Do not generate images of classmates, teachers, or strangers in compromising scenarios. Prefer fictional subjects or stock you have rights to use.

Lighter touch than the audio ethics deep-dive, same principle: capability ≠ permission. If you would not paste someone’s face on a billboard without asking, do not do it with diffusion.

Check your knowledge: ethics scenario

Question: A classmate asks you to generate a realistic photo of them “at a party they missed” to post online. What’s wrong, and what do you do?

Show answer

Non-consensual likeness + potential misinformation. Decline; explain consent and deepfake risks; suggest a clearly labeled fictional avatar or a real photo they provide with permission.
